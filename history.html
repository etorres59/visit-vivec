The History of Vivec City

A Timeline Through the Ages of Tamriel

Founding of Vivec City (Mythic roots)

According to Vivec's 36 Lessons, the city's origin is tied to mythic events during the era of Resdaynia — an ancient name for Morrowind before the Tribunal rose to power. The founding occurs sometime after the Chimer settled Morrowind but before the First Era.

Red Mountain Eruption

The first major eruption of Red Mountain reshaped much of Vvardenfell. It is sometime after this that Morrowind gained its current name and the Tribunal consolidated power.

Construction of Vivec City as Tribunal Center

Vivec City was built around the Ministry of Truth and the Palace of Vivec, becoming a spiritual, political, and economic capital of Vvardenfell under Tribunal Temple control.

ESO

During this time, Vivec still ruled from his palace. The city was bustling with commerce, pilgrims, and politics. The Redoran, Hlaalu, and Telvanni Cantons held administrative centers and noble estates. A mysterious affliction affected Vivec's divine powers, which the Vestige helped investigate and resolve.

Jiub Becomes a Saint

Jiub, the famed Dunmer who eliminated 76 cliff racers, was granted sainthood by Vivec himself in a public ceremony at the Palace of Vivec—possibly one of the last recorded audiences with the god before his disappearance.

TES 3

By this time, the Tribunal's power wanes, and Vivec remains the only active member. The Nerevarine visits Vivec City and gains audience with Lord Vivec. The city serves as a central hub for quests, faction politics, and religious pilgrimage.

Vivec's Disappearance & Collapse of the Ministry of Truth

After the Oblivion Crisis and the Tribunal's fall, Vivec disappears. Without his divine will holding it in place, the Ministry of Truth (a suspended moon-like rock) crashes into the city. While some sources claim the Daedric Prince Baar Dau was redirected or destroyed, much of Vivec City was devastated. This event helped trigger the Red Year (eruption of Red Mountain in 4E 5), reshaping Vvardenfell and Morrowind entirely.

Culture

Morrowind is home to three main cultural groups: the Dunmer Great Houses, the Ashlanders, and the Imperials. The Ashlanders are nomadic, living in small, portable yurts. The Imperials, being more recent settlers, construct towns like Pelagiad with western-style homes, stone forts, and organized city layouts. Despite these influences, the Dunmer Great Houses remain culturally dominant, each preserving distinct traditions, architecture, and societal structures rooted in centuries of history.

Vivec City, once a shining example of Dunmeri urban civilization, was a thriving metropolis that embodied religious devotion, architectural ingenuity, and cosmopolitan commerce. Its layout consisted of massive cantons—self-contained districts rising from Norvayn Bay—each dedicated to a Great House, saint, or social function. Saint Delyn and Saint Olms Cantons were the population centers, housing commoners in exterior apartments while merchants and craftspeople worked inside. These cantons hosted guild halls for brewers, fishmongers, glassworkers, potters, tailors, tanners, and miners, who collectively defined the island’s local industries. For example, the rust funnelcap paint used across the island was crafted in Vivec by grinding funnelcap mushrooms into a potent paste.

Transportation was largely aquatic, with small gondolas ferrying passengers between cantons via an intricate network of canals. Larger vessels and silt striders arrived at the North Landing. The city was also home to the Arena Canton, a major cultural venue that hosted gladiatorial combat, theatrical performances, and city-wide events. The famed local team, the Vivec City Cliff Racers, once competed in the pan-Tamrielic Dragon Games, further cementing the city's place in popular entertainment.

The Great House cantons—Hlaalu, Redoran, and Telvanni—functioned as powerful centers of political and economic activity. Each housed its own administrative offices, treasuries, and court cells. House Redoran's canton was home to noble clans like the Dralor and Saren families. House Telvanni, ever eccentric, rented their compound from the Temple, populating it with traditional Velothi towers instead of mushroom towers. The upper levels offered magical services, while the lower levels held monsters and slaves—reflecting their controversial practices.

Vivec City was also notable for its degree of multiculturalism, rare in a land typically suspicious of outlanders. The Foreign Quarter, located at the city’s northern edge, housed traders, visitors, and Imperial agents. It even featured the Foreign Quarter Tomb—an expansive burial site for non-Dunmer, which was uncommon in more provincial areas of Morrowind. Meanwhile, the Hlaalu Compound included ancestral vaults, likely serving ceremonial or burial purposes for House Hlaalu elites.

Religious devotion was ever-present. Pilgrims journeyed to the city to visit the High Fane, offer prayers at the Palace of Vivec, or marvel at the suspended meteor Baar Dau—also known as the Ministry of Truth. Access to Vivec himself was rare, reserved for only the most devout. Legend holds that Jiub, famed for slaying seventy-six cliff racers, was among the last to be granted an audience before the god mysteriously vanished.

Religion

Daedra Worship

Originally, the Chimer worshiped several Daedric Princes after being led to Morrowind by Veloth and Boethiah. Azura, Boethiah, and Mephala—known as the "Good Daedra"—were especially revered. They influenced philosophy, politics, and culture, including the foundation of the Great House system. Other Daedra like Malacath, Molag Bal, Mehrunes Dagon, and Sheogorath were seen as dangerous but important testing figures.

The Tribunal and House of Troubles

When the Tribunal—Almalexia, Vivec, and Sotha Sil—rose to godhood, worship of the Daedra was suppressed. The Good Daedra were rebranded as the "Anticipations" of the Tribunal, while the more chaotic Daedra became the "House of Troubles." These figures were cast as tempters and challenges, especially to discourage traditional Daedra worship among the settled Dunmer.

Tribunal Temple

The Tribunal Temple centered worship on the living gods: Almalexia, Vivec, and Sotha Sil, known together as ALMSIVI. Each had patron roles: Almalexia for healers, Vivec for artists and warriors, and Sotha Sil for inventors and mages. Their acts, like stopping Baar Dau or banishing Mehrunes Dagon, helped solidify their place in Dunmer society as protectors and spiritual leaders.

Fall of the Tribunal

After the Nerevarine destroyed the enchantments tied to the Heart of Lorkhan, the Tribunal’s power faded. Almalexia, driven mad by her loss of divinity, killed Sotha Sil and was in turn killed by the Nerevarine. Vivec eventually disappeared, with his fate left unknown.

The Reclamations

Following the catastrophic Red Year, the Dunmer lost faith in the Tribunal. A religious shift led by the Dissident Priests restored worship of Azura, Boethiah, and Mephala. These Daedra became known as the Reclamations, while the Tribunal were downgraded to saints. The Ashlanders were honored for preserving this original form of worship.
